The Domestic Politics of Trade Conditionality: Firm-level Foundations of U.S. Interests in International Financial Institutions

Ha, Hyunsoo

Paper

Existing research shows that IMF and World Bank lending often reflects the preferences of powerful creditor states, but it leaves the domestic origins of those preferences less understood. This paper examines U.S. exporters as a source of creditor-state preferences toward trade-related conditionality. I argue that exporters view IMF and World Bank conditionality as an alternative channel for advancing foreign market liberalization. By reducing trade barriers in borrowing countries without requiring reciprocal liberalization, such conditionality allows exporters to pursue market access while avoiding domestic political opposition. Using firm-level export and lobbying data, I find that U.S. firms’ export exposure is positively associated with World Bank-related lobbying. Exporters are also more likely to direct World Bank-related lobbying toward the Department of the Treasury, the agency responsible for coordinating U.S. policy toward these institutions. The paper contributes by identifying domestic firm-level foundations of creditor-state interests in international financial institutions.

Isolated Partners: Foreign Direct Investment among Sanctioned States

Ha, Hyunsoo, and Joonseok Yang

  • Presented at Korean Association of International Studies (KAIS) Summer Conference 2026
Paper

How do economic sanctions reshape firms’ foreign investment incentives? While existing studies have advanced divergent expectations about whether sanctions constitute risks or opportunities for foreign investors, they have shown little interest in identifying the conditions under which such discrepancies arise. This study examines firms’ prior exposure to sanctions in their home countries as an institutional condition influencing foreign investment incentives. We argue that firms from sanctioned countries would view other sanctioned countries as more viable investment destinations, with their prior exposure lowering the costs of operating under similar constraints. Using project-level FDI data on 195 countries from 2003 to 2023, we present robust evidence that firms from sanctioned countries are more likely to initiate FDI projects in other sanctioned countries. This study contributes by advancing our understanding of firm-level FDI incentives and sanctions effectiveness, uncovering an overlooked form of private sanctions-busting.

Global Innovation Networks and Political Heterogeneity: Evidence from Cross-border Co-patents

Ha, Hyunsoo, and Seong Hun Yoo

  • Presented at International Studies Association (ISA) Annual Convention 2026
  • Presented at American Political Science Association (APSA) Annual Meeting 2025
Paper

Technological innovation increasingly depends on cross-border collaborations. While much work in international political economy emphasizes institutional similarity as a foundation for deep economic integration, the global network of cross-border joint patent filings points to a puzzle: innovation collaboration often emerges between politically dissimilar countries. We revisit the similarity hypothesis and argue that regime differences can act as drivers of cross-border co-patenting through two mechanisms: (1) technological complementarity, where firms bridge democratic advantages in basic research with autocratic capabilities in resource mobilization and commercialization; and (2) strategic risk mitigation, where co-patenting serves as a private governance mechanism to secure property rights in high-risk environments. Using cross-border patent collaboration data from 116 countries between 1977 and 2018, we find robust evidence that cross-border co-patenting is systematically more prevalent between politically heterogeneous country pairs. These findings carry important implications for international cooperation in strategic technology.
